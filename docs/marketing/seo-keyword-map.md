# Jaspen SEO Keyword Map

Last updated: 2026-07-14

## Purpose and method

This map ties existing (and candidate future) pages to the search terms a person facing a real decision would actually type. It is directional, not a volume report: I don't have access to a keyword-volume tool (Ahrefs, Semrush, Google Search Console) in this environment, so no search-volume or difficulty numbers below are real data — treat them as unverified until checked against one of those tools. What I did do is confirm, via live web search, that each cluster below reflects real, common search phrasing (not a guess at what people "might" search) — sources are listed at the bottom of each cluster.

The organizing principle, per Jaspen's positioning: target people who are in the middle of a real decision and need to act on it, not general "productivity tool" browsers. That means favoring specific, intent-rich phrases over head terms Jaspen has no authority to compete for yet (e.g., not "project management software," which is owned by Asana/Monday/Jira; instead "turn a decision into an execution plan," which is closer to what Jaspen actually does).

## Cluster 1: Decision scoring and evaluation

**Maps to:** `/pages/jaspen-score`

| Intent | Example phrases | Funnel stage |
|---|---|---|
| Informational | weighted decision matrix, weighted scoring model, how to score a decision, decision matrix template | Awareness / consideration |
| Informational (branded fit) | how to compare options objectively, evidence-based decision making, decision confidence scoring | Consideration |

This cluster is already Jaspen's strongest page (most complete content, was the only page with schema markup, highest sitemap priority). It lines up closely with an established, real search category: "weighted scoring model" and "weighted decision matrix" are both active, well-documented search terms with existing competitor content (airfocus, ProductSchool, Tempo). Jaspen's differentiation — confidence caps, reproducibility, that the AI proposes but doesn't decide — is a legitimate angle competitors in this space don't cover.

Sources: [airfocus – Weighted decision matrix](https://airfocus.com/blog/weighted-decision-matrix-prioritization/), [ProductSchool – Weighted Scoring Model](https://productschool.com/blog/product-fundamentals/weighted-scoring-model), [Tempo – Weighted scoring model guide](https://www.tempo.io/blog/weighted-scoring-model)

## Cluster 2: Decision-to-execution / strategy execution

**Maps to:** `/pages/jaspen`, `/pages/project-management`

| Intent | Example phrases | Funnel stage |
|---|---|---|
| Informational | how to turn a decision into a plan, strategy execution software, connecting strategy to delivery | Consideration |
| Comparison / evaluation | strategy execution vs project management software, tools that connect decisions to delivery | Decision |

Real search confirms "strategy execution software" is an established category with named competitors (Elate, ClearPoint, AchieveIt, Cascade, Mooncamp) who focus on leadership operating rhythm — objectives, KPIs, reporting cadence. Jaspen's actual differentiation, per its own page copy, is narrower and more concrete: it generates the execution plan directly from the decision that produced it, so nothing is "translated" by hand. That's a specific, defensible angle worth owning rather than competing head-on with established strategy-execution platforms on their broader term.

Sources: [ClearPoint – Choosing strategy execution software](https://www.clearpointstrategy.com/blog/how-to-choose-strategy-execution-software), [G2 – Strategic Planning and Execution category](https://www.g2.com/categories/strategic-planning-and-execution)

## Cluster 3: Decision intelligence / AI for decisions

**Maps to:** `/pages/jaspen` (secondary), homepage

| Intent | Example phrases | Funnel stage |
|---|---|---|
| Informational | AI decision intelligence, AI tool for executive decisions, agentic AI for decision making | Awareness |

This is a real, fast-moving category (Quantive, Aera, NexStrat.AI all surfaced), but it's also the most crowded and the most abstract — most competitor content here is high-level and vendor-agnostic rather than product-specific. I'd treat this as a supporting cluster (useful for the homepage's broader positioning) rather than a page to build a dedicated new landing page around yet, since Jaspen's concrete, provable differentiation (reproducible scoring, confidence caps, generated execution plans) is easier to substantiate on the narrower Cluster 1 and 2 terms than on "decision intelligence" broadly.

Sources: [Quantive – AI for decision-making](https://quantive.com/resources/articles/ai-for-decision-making), [Rapidops – Decision intelligence tools](https://www.rapidops.com/blog/best-decision-intelligence-tools/)

## Cluster 4: Integration / connector terms

**Maps to:** `/pages/jaspen-in-jira`, `/pages/jaspen-in-smartsheets`, `/pages/resources/connectors`

| Intent | Example phrases | Funnel stage |
|---|---|---|
| Navigational / transactional | Jaspen Jira integration, Jaspen Smartsheet integration, sync execution plan to Jira | Decision |
| Informational | Jira and Smartsheet together, execution plan to sprint tracking | Consideration |

Lower search volume than Clusters 1–3, but high intent — someone searching "Jaspen Jira integration" is already evaluating the product, not browsing. This is the cluster to defend well rather than expand; the recent internal-linking fix (Connectors and Tutorials now link to both integration pages) directly supports it.

## What I'd avoid targeting directly

- **"Project management software"** and similar single-word head terms — owned by Asana, Monday, Jira, ClickUp. No realistic path to rank, and it would pull Jaspen's positioning away from decisions toward generic PM, which isn't the audience you want.
- **"AI tool"** as a standalone phrase — too broad, too commoditized, attracts the "curious browser" audience rather than someone facing a real decision.

## Open question for new landing pages (next recommendation, not yet actioned)

Clusters 1 and 2 both point toward a possible new page: a page built around "weighted decision matrix" or "decision matrix template" as its own standalone educational resource (distinct from the Jaspen Score product page), similar to how competitors in that space (airfocus, Tempo) rank with dedicated guide content. I'd want to evaluate this properly as its own recommendation rather than fold it in here, since a new page is a bigger commitment than a metadata or linking fix.
