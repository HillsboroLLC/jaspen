# The Organizational Moat

Jaspen compounds because decisions compound.

Most software captures activity: messages, tasks, files, meetings, tickets, notes, and dashboards. Jaspen captures the reasoning behind decisions and turns that reasoning into reusable organizational knowledge.

Be precise about what the moat is. Deterministic scoring, evidence grading, and the Constitution are **trust mechanisms** — necessary, differentiating, and, like all features, ultimately copyable. The moat is what those mechanisms make possible: **years of accumulated Decision Records, outcome history, and organizational memory** that no competitor — and no customer starting over — can rebuild quickly. Trust mechanisms earn the deposits. The accumulated memory is the vault.

## Decision Records

Every completed decision should be able to become a private, customer-owned Decision Record.

The record preserves the decision statement, objectives, criteria, weights, alternatives, evidence, confidence, assumptions, recommendation, execution plan, outcome, and lessons learned.

This creates a durable memory of what the organization believed at the moment of choice.

## Decision Library

When appropriate, explicitly permitted, and anonymized, selected Decision Records can become a public Decision Library.

The library turns prior decisions into onboarding material, starter examples, decision kits, reusable frameworks, education, marketing examples, and future evaluation resources.

The library is not just content. It is accumulated decision knowledge.

## Organizational Memory

Organizations forget why they made decisions.

People leave. Priorities change. Decks get buried. Meeting context disappears. Decisions become stories, and stories become politics.

Jaspen's Decision Record foundation gives the organization a place to preserve the reasoning itself: the criteria, evidence, confidence, assumptions, and outcome.

## Pattern Discovery

Across many governed Decision Records, patterns may begin to emerge.

Examples:

- Which criteria repeatedly predict better outcomes.
- Which assumptions fail most often.
- Which evidence changes recommendations.
- Which teams overestimate execution readiness.
- Which risks are consistently underweighted.
- Which confidence grades are well calibrated.
- Which decisions succeed despite lower initial scores because of strategic locks.

Pattern Discovery is a future capability. It does not make decisions for the organization. It should improve the conversation around future decisions.

## Institutional Knowledge

Institutional knowledge is usually trapped in experienced people.

Jaspen can help translate that knowledge into structured records that are easier to inspect, teach, reuse, and challenge. This matters for onboarding, succession, governance, strategy reviews, and cross-functional alignment.

## Why the Product Becomes More Valuable Over Time

The first Jaspen decision creates clarity.

The tenth can create reusable comparison.

The hundredth may begin to reveal how the organization thinks.

The thousandth can become a governed body of decision intelligence: patterns, calibration, templates, examples, and lessons that help future teams make better choices without surrendering judgment.

## The Moat Is Not Only Data

The moat is the structure of the data.

Raw chat logs are hard to compare. Documents are hard to aggregate. Meeting notes are hard to trust. Decision Records are designed to be comparable because they preserve the same categories of judgment: criteria, weights, evidence, confidence, recommendation, outcome, and learning.

That structure makes learning possible.

## Why Competitors Cannot Easily Copy It

Features can be copied. A capable competitor could rebuild deterministic scoring, evidence grading, and confidence caps within a few quarters — and honestly, the market would be better if they did.

What cannot be copied on any schedule:

- **Accumulated Decision Records with outcomes.** A body of real decisions — each carrying what was believed, what was chosen, and what reality taught months later — takes years to exist. There is no synthetic substitute and no shortcut: outcome history only accumulates at the speed of reality.
- **Customer switching costs that feel like loyalty.** An organization's decision memory lives in its records. Leaving means abandoning the institution's memory of its own judgment — a different magnitude of loss than exporting a chat history.
- **Calibration earned in public.** Confidence numbers become credible only when outcome history shows the 70s behaved like 70s. That trust is accumulated, not announced, and every year of honest calibration — including the visible misses — widens the gap.
- **A governed, consented corpus.** Custody rings, permissioning, and anonymization take time to operationalize and longer to be believed by enterprises.

The constitutional architecture is what makes customers willing to deposit their decisions in the first place. The deposits, compounding over years, are the moat.
