# Elevator Pitches

These are positioning foundations, not final advertising scripts.

## 30 Seconds

Jaspen is a Decision Intelligence platform, not an AI chatbot. It helps people and organizations turn messy context into structured, explainable decisions. AI helps interview the user and judge evidence, but the scoring is deterministic, confidence is tied to evidence, and the rubric belongs to the user. The output is not just an answer — it is the full decision artifact, preserved through the canonical Decision Record foundation as a private record the customer can learn from. General AI gives you an answer. Jaspen helps you make the call you can defend.

## 1 Minute

Most AI tools are optimized to give you a fluent answer. Jaspen is built to help you make a decision you can explain, defend, execute, and learn from.

The product starts with conversation because decisions are messy. But it does not stop at chat. Jaspen turns context into criteria, weights, alternatives, evidence, assumptions, confidence, deterministic scoring, trade-off analysis, and execution planning. The user owns the rubric and the final decision. The AI helps judge evidence and ask better questions. Code performs the math.

With explicit opt-in and anonymization, selected records may later support a public Decision Library. Jaspen helps organizations improve how they decide, not just how they talk to AI.

## 3 Minutes

Jaspen exists because organizations do not lack opinions, data, or AI-generated text. They lack a repeatable way to turn ambiguous context into decisions people can stand behind.

Most general AI assistants are conversation systems. They can produce helpful answers, but the answer is often a block of language. Jaspen is a decision system. It uses AI conversation as an intake layer, then moves the user into structured decision analysis.

The core architecture separates roles. The human owns the criteria, weights, strategic locks, and final decision. The AI helps interview, synthesize, and judge evidence. Code performs the scoring math. That means published numbers are not model-generated vibes. They are deterministic rollups over visible criteria, weights, evidence, and confidence.

Confidence matters because decisions are rarely made with complete information. Jaspen does not block progress when evidence is incomplete. It proceeds with labeled assumptions and honest confidence. Weak evidence caps contribution; better evidence raises confidence. This makes uncertainty visible instead of hiding it inside persuasive prose.

The output is a decision artifact: the decision statement, alternatives, criteria, weights, evidence, assumptions, score decomposition, recommendation, and execution plan. The long-term artifact is the Decision Record, which captures what was decided, why, what happened, and what was learned.

That is where Jaspen becomes more than a decision tool. Decision Records can become organizational memory. With proper custody, explicit opt-in, and anonymization, some records may become a public Decision Library. Separately, governed non-identifying records can support future evaluation, calibration, and learning. Across many decisions, organizations may eventually discover patterns: which assumptions fail, which criteria matter, which evidence changes outcomes, and where confidence is miscalibrated.

Jaspen does not replace judgment. It gives judgment a structure, a record, and a way to improve.

## 10 Minute Investor Presentation

### 1. The Problem

Organizations make expensive decisions through fragmented context: meetings, decks, spreadsheets, chat threads, dashboards, documents, and stakeholder opinions.

The decision itself often disappears. Teams remember the outcome, but not the reasoning: what criteria mattered, what evidence existed, which assumptions were made, why confidence was high or low, and what changed later.

AI assistants make this easier to talk about, but they do not solve the decision architecture problem by themselves. A fluent answer is not the same as a defensible decision.

### 2. The Category

Jaspen is building a Decision Intelligence platform.

Decision Intelligence sits between AI conversation, analytics, planning, and organizational knowledge. It is not a chatbot category. It is a system for making, preserving, and learning from decisions.

The term itself is not new — analysts have described a Decision Intelligence category rooted in data-science tooling for years. Jaspen re-founds it: where the first generation tried to compute better decisions from data, Jaspen makes decisions governable — judgment explicit, math deterministic, records owned, learning transparent.

### 3. The Product

Jaspen starts in conversation. A user describes a decision, pastes context, or brings data. Jaspen asks focused questions and helps structure the decision.

Then the platform creates decision analysis: alternatives, criteria, weights, evidence, assumptions, confidence, score decomposition, recommendation, and execution plan.

The output is a decision artifact, not only a response.

The canonical example: a job offer weighed against staying put. The user owns the weights; evidence is graded (offer terms are self-reported — medium; "a promotion is possible" — low); confidence caps what thin evidence can contribute; and code computes the rollup. Staying scores 72, the offer 58, at medium confidence — every point decomposable, and "what would raise the score" has a concrete answer. The same mechanism, unchanged, scores a board's capital allocation.

### 4. The Architectural Difference

Jaspen separates human judgment, AI judgment, and code calculation.

The human owns the rubric and decision. AI helps judge evidence and synthesize reasoning. Code calculates the scoring mechanics. Confidence caps prevent unsupported optimism from dominating the result.

This architecture is designed to resist unsupported AI conclusions without claiming hallucinations are impossible.

### 5. The Trust Model

Trust is built through the Constitution, deterministic scoring, evidence grading, confidence calibration, transparent reasoning, user-owned rubrics, labeled assumptions, and custody rings for Decision Records.

Every score should decompose. Every assumption should be visible. Every customer record should have clear custody.

### 6. The Compounding Asset

The long-term asset is the Decision Record.

The canonical Decision Record foundation lets completed decisions become structured organizational memory. Across many governed records, Jaspen can eventually support a Decision Library, pattern discovery, confidence calibration, and future Decision Intelligence.

The more an organization uses Jaspen, the more it can learn from its own decision history.

### 7. The Market Expansion Path

Individuals use Jaspen for high-stakes personal decisions.

Solopreneurs use it as a structured advisor.

Small businesses use it to prioritize with less chaos.

Mid-market teams use it for portfolio sequencing and cross-functional alignment.

Enterprises use it for governance, auditability, institutional memory, and decision quality at scale.

The same mechanism scales because the framework encodes decision form, not domain content.

### 8. The Moat

The moat is not raw chat data, and it is not the scoring engine — features can be copied.

The moat is accumulated, structured decision knowledge: criteria, weights, evidence, confidence, assumptions, outcomes, and lessons learned, compounding at the speed of reality. Outcome history cannot be synthesized, and an organization's decision memory living in its records is a switching cost that feels like loyalty.

Over time, Jaspen becomes the system of record for why decisions were made — and leaving means leaving the institution's memory of its own judgment behind.

### 9. The Vision

Jaspen's vision is to help organizations become Learning Systems for decisions.

Not automatic decision machines. Not AI oracles. Learning systems where people remain accountable, rubrics remain user-owned, and accumulated decision history improves future guidance.

### 10. The Closing Line

Jaspen does not replace human judgment. It gives human judgment structure, evidence, confidence, memory, and a way to improve over time.

Make the call you can defend.
