# The Decision Intelligence Platform

Jaspen's long-term architecture is a Learning System for decisions.

It begins with a single decision and compounds into organizational intelligence.

```text
Decision
  ->
Conversation
  ->
Decision Analysis
  ->
Decision Record
  ->
Decision Library
  ->
Pattern Discovery
  ->
Decision Intelligence
```

Each layer exists for a reason. None of the layers replaces human judgment. Each layer makes judgment more structured, transparent, and reusable.

## The Category Jaspen Is Re-Founding

Decision Intelligence is not a new term. Analysts have described a Decision Intelligence category for years, and its first generation grew out of data science: decision modeling, optimization, and machine-learning pipelines applied to enterprise data. That lineage produced real value — and a familiar limitation. The intelligence lived in models and dashboards, judgment stayed implicit, and the human's criteria were rarely first-class objects anyone could inspect or own.

Jaspen respects that work and takes a different architectural path. Where traditional Decision Intelligence tried to compute better decisions **from data**, Jaspen makes decisions **governable**:

- Judgment is explicit — criteria and weights are user-owned objects, not model internals.
- Calculation is deterministic and inspectable, not a model output.
- Evidence and confidence are graded, capped, and visible.
- Every decision can become a durable, human-readable record with clear custody.
- Learning accumulates transparently, under a written Constitution.

The category's name was right. Jaspen's contribution is a constitutional, human-governed architecture for it.

## 1. Decision

The unit of value is the decision.

The decision may be personal, operational, strategic, financial, organizational, or enterprise-wide. It may involve one option, several alternatives, competing priorities, incomplete evidence, stakeholders, trade-offs, risks, constraints, and execution requirements.

Jaspen starts here because the customer does not need another place to talk. The customer needs a better way to decide.

## 2. Conversation

Conversation is the intake layer.

It lets the user describe the situation naturally, paste notes, bring partial context, and answer focused follow-up questions. The AI partner helps transform ambiguity into structure without forcing the user to begin with a rigid form.

This layer exists because most valuable decisions begin messy.

## 3. Decision Analysis

Decision Analysis is the active product layer that turns context into a structured evaluation.

This includes the decision statement, alternatives, objectives, criteria, weights, evidence, assumptions, confidence, trade-offs, score decomposition, recommendation, and execution implications.

This layer exists because a decision is only defensible when the reasoning can be inspected.

## 4. Decision Record

The canonical Decision Record is the durable artifact now being formalized as the foundation for the Learning System.

It preserves what was decided, why it was decided, what evidence was used, which assumptions mattered, what confidence was warranted, what plan followed, what outcome occurred, and what lessons were learned.

This layer exists because organizations lose value when decisions disappear into meetings, decks, chat threads, and memory.

## 5. Decision Library

Decision Records may become a curated Decision Library in the future only when appropriate custody and permission allow it.

The library may support onboarding, starter examples, decision kits, customer education, reusable frameworks, SEO, AI search optimization, and future evaluation. Public library entries require explicit permission and anonymization or approved attribution.

This layer exists because useful decisions can become reusable knowledge without forcing private customer records into public use.

## 6. Pattern Discovery

Across many governed Decision Records, Jaspen can eventually help discover patterns.

Patterns may include common success factors, common failure modes, hidden trade-offs, sensitivity relationships, recurring blind spots, evidence that changes outcomes, confidence calibration trends, and long-term outcome signals.

This future layer exists because individual decisions are valuable, but repeated decisions may reveal the shape of an organization's judgment.

Pattern Discovery never replaces the user. It informs the next decision.

Machine learning may eventually help find these patterns; it never rewrites the rules of the system it serves (see Trust Architecture: "Where Machine Learning Fits").

## 7. Decision Intelligence

Decision Intelligence is the future application of accumulated learning to future decisions.

It means future users can benefit from previous decisions without surrendering control. Jaspen may eventually say things like: "In similar decisions, teams that weighted manager quality more heavily saw better long-term outcomes."

The user still owns the criteria, weights, alternatives, final decision, and accountability.

This layer exists because organizations should get smarter from decisions they have already paid to make.

## The Canonical Principle

There is one canonical Decision Record schema and multiple custody levels.

The schema preserves the same kind of decision knowledge. Custody determines how that knowledge may be used: private customer memory, explicitly permitted and anonymized public library content, or governed non-identifying internal evaluation and calibration. One schema does not imply one shared data pool.

## What This Architecture Makes Possible

Jaspen can serve a solo user making one important choice and an enterprise trying to improve decision quality across teams.

The same mechanism scales because the methodology encodes form, not domain: criteria, weights, evidence, confidence, recommendation, execution, outcome, and learning.
