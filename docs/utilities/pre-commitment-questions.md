# Top 25 Pre-Commitment Questions — Question First, No Tools Yet

Last updated: 2026-07-15
Status: research only. No utility design, no architecture changes. Per your instruction, this stops at the question — the tool gets designed after you've reacted to this list.

## The test applied

Not "what would make a good calculator" — "what question keeps someone awake the night before they commit real resources, that they'd gladly spend three minutes on if it gave them a real answer." Measurements (confidence, overrun percentage, decision style) are what Jaspen can compute *after* someone engages. They are not what makes someone stop scrolling. A problem does that.

Each question below is marked **strong** where I found direct language or data backing it in this project's research, or **synthesized** where it's a reasonable extrapolation from adjacent findings rather than something I found people asking in those words. I'm keeping that distinction visible rather than presenting every question as equally evidenced.

## Before committing — "should we even do this?"

1. **Are we solving the right problem, or just the most visible symptom of it?** — *strong.* HBR's own research found 85% of C-suite executives admit their organization is bad at problem diagnosis, and 87% say it carries real cost.
2. **Why are we doing this now, and what are we saying no to by doing it?** — *strong.* Near-exact phrasing found in HBR/FranklinCovey pre-project checklists.
3. **Is this actually worth what it's going to cost us?** — *strong.* Directly matches "is this project worth investing in" research cluster.
4. **What are we assuming that we haven't actually verified?** — *strong.* Ties directly to the project-blind-spots research and is the one question your own product's confidence-grading mechanism is built to answer honestly.
5. **What are we overlooking that we'll wish we'd caught before we started?** — *strong.* Matches the "blind spots in project management" research cluster almost word for word.
6. **Do we actually have the time, people, and budget this will take — not the plan's version of it?** — *strong.* Direct match to "questions to ask before starting a project" research.
7. **Do we actually have the people who need to be on board, on board?** — *strong.* Same cluster — stakeholder buy-in is named repeatedly as a make-or-break precondition.
8. **Are we about to commit resources before we're actually ready?** — *synthesized* from the initiative-readiness research, reworded as the anxious version rather than the checklist version.
9. **Is this the most valuable thing we could be doing with these resources right now?** — *strong.* Matches the portfolio-prioritization / "resources going to low-value initiatives" research directly.
10. **If this goes wrong, what will we wish we had asked before we started?** — *synthesized* — a reasonable pre-mortem framing, not a literal phrase I found in search results.

## Mid-flight — "is this going off the rails?"

11. **Is this actually going to deliver what we told leadership it would?** — *strong.* Directly reflects the benefits-realization research ("stakeholders know the project shipped, but aren't sure the business is better off").
12. **Why do our projects keep coming in over budget or behind schedule, no matter how carefully we plan?** — *strong.* Backed by the most quoted stats in this entire research effort (91.5% of large projects over budget/schedule, PMI's 43% figure).
13. **Are we quietly redoing work someone else in the organization already did?** — *strong.* Direct match to the duplication-of-effort research.
14. **Is this initiative underperforming, and if so, why?** — *strong.* Direct match to the underperforming-initiatives research cluster.
15. **Are we still doing this because it's right, or because we've already spent too much to stop?** — *strong.* Matches the sunk-cost-fallacy research almost exactly.
16. **Should we kill this, or push through?** — *strong.* Same cluster; "20 signs it's time to rethink a project" is a real, widely-shared piece.
17. **How would we even know this was going badly before it's too late to fix?** — *synthesized* from the early-warning-signs research, reframed as the anxious question instead of the listicle title.

## After the decision — memory, accountability, repetition

18. **Why can't anyone here explain why we made this decision six months ago?** — *strong.* Near-verbatim match to real decision-log research ("a month later, teams remember the outcome but not the reasoning").
19. **Will this reasoning still make sense to someone new a year from now?** — *synthesized*, a close variant of #18.
20. **What happens to everything we learned once this project is over?** — *strong.* Directly matches the lessons-learned research (why teams skip it, why the insight evaporates).
21. **If the person who understands this best left tomorrow, would we be in real trouble?** — *strong.* Backed by the tribal/institutional-knowledge research (the real example of a $2.3M scramble to replace a retiring machinist's knowledge).
22. **Why do we keep having the same argument about the same decision?** — *strong.* Matches existing competitor content almost exactly, and is a direct hit on your own Confidence pillar.

## Leadership and board-facing

23. **How do I defend this decision if it doesn't work out?** — *strong.* Matches the board decision-papers research directly.
24. **Are we going to look back on this as an obvious mistake?** — *synthesized* — emotionally real, but I didn't find this exact phrasing in search results, only adjacent hindsight-bias and failure-rate content.
25. **Is our organization actually good at making decisions like this, or do we just feel like we are?** — *strong.* Ties to the organizational-maturity-assessment research and is close to the deepest, most identity-level version of the whole list.

## What stands out, without yet designing anything

The questions with the strongest pull, by my read, are the ones that are least about the initiative and most about the organization's *pattern*: #1 (solving the right problem), #12 (why do we always overrun), #18 (why can't we explain past decisions), and #25 (are we actually good at this). Those four aren't asking about one project — they're asking "is this us, or is this bad luck," which is a much more anxious, much more identity-level question than any single-project calculator could ever answer. That may be where the real flagship is hiding, exactly as you suspected — but I'm stopping here, at the question, as instructed.
