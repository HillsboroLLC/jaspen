# Business Outcome and Operational Pain Research

Last updated: 2026-07-15
Status: research only. Nothing built, content architecture not yet revised, per your instruction.

## Method

Same research discipline as the prior two passes — live search, real phrasing, existing tools checked before assuming a gap exists — now focused exclusively on the twelve outcome/pain themes you listed rather than decision-mechanism language.

## Findings by theme

### Projects not delivering expected results / poor project ROI
Carried forward from the first research pass — Standish/PMI failure-rate stats (65-71%), Bain's 88% transformation-failure figure, McKinsey's persistent 30% success rate. Existing tools are ROI-formula guides, not forward-looking diagnostics. See below for the strongest new angle on this theme.

### Initiatives underperforming
- **Exact language:** "underperforming initiatives," "why priorities agreed at executive level fail to convert into delivery"
- **Format sought:** diagnostic guides (a real concept worth noting: an "Alignment Heatmap" — mapping company priorities against active initiatives to show where work supports strategy, duplicates other work, or has no real backing — appears in consulting content as a practitioner framework, not as a public interactive tool)
- **Driver:** business pain, senior-leadership-level
- **Inputs an honest utility would need:** what outcome is underperforming, what initiatives are meant to serve it, how clearly ownership and metrics are defined
- **Honest output:** a diagnostic pattern-match ("your initiative shows 3 of the 5 common underperformance patterns"), never a prediction that the initiative will fail
- **Crowded or underserved:** underserved as an interactive tool — everything found is consulting-framework content, not something self-serve

### Projects going over budget
- **Exact language:** "cost overrun," "budget overrun," "projects over budget statistics"
- **Format sought:** calculator/statistics — **a real, direct competitor exists**: budgetoverrun.com offers overrun statistics and a calculator
- **Driver:** business pain, extremely recognizable (this may be the single most universally-feared project statistic there is)
- **Numbers worth knowing:** 91.5% of large projects go over budget, schedule, or both; PMI cites 43% exceeding original budget with a mean overrun near 27-62% depending on the study; IT projects with overruns above 50% average a 447% overrun
- **Inputs an honest utility would need:** project size, requirements clarity, team's estimating track record, how much of the plan rests on assumed vs. evidenced numbers
- **Honest output:** a reference-class range ("projects like this typically overrun by X-Y%, based on published industry data"), explicitly not a prediction of this specific project's outcome
- **Crowded or underserved:** a competitor exists but reads as generic stats-plus-calculator, not diagnostic. Real differentiation available: tie the *exposure* to which specific inputs are assumed rather than evidenced — something a generic overrun calculator can't do because it has no underlying evidence-grading mechanism.

### Rework and duplicated effort
- **Exact language:** "duplication of effort," "reinventing the wheel," "teams redoing work that already exists"
- **Format sought:** advice articles (nBold, ClickUp, Sunsama) — no interactive calculator found for general (non-engineering) duplicated work
- **Driver:** business pain, often invisible until named
- **Inputs:** team size, estimated hours/week on rework or recreated work, rough sense of how often "did we already do this?" comes up
- **Honest output:** an estimated hours/cost range, clearly labeled as a rough estimate from self-reported inputs
- **Crowded or underserved:** underserved specifically for cross-team/organizational duplication (distinct from the engineering-specific rework tools found in the first research pass)

### Wasted project resources / resources committed to low-value initiatives
- **Exact language:** "initiative overload," "resource misallocation," "prioritize projects based on strategic value instead of urgency"
- **Format sought:** prioritization matrices and weighted-scoring tools — **this space is crowded**: RICE, ICE, value-vs-complexity matrices, and dedicated tools like a "Strategic Prioritization Tool" (toolsrail.com) already exist and are well-established in product/portfolio management
- **Driver:** business pain, portfolio/PMO-level
- **Crowded or underserved:** crowded. A generic version adds little. The only differentiated angle is tying the score to evidence quality and confidence (why a "high value" initiative might actually be a guess dressed up as a number) rather than another value/effort quadrant — otherwise, skip this one.

### Failed transformation initiatives
- **Exact language:** "why transformations fail," direct citation of Bain's 88% and McKinsey's 70% failure figures across dozens of consultancy and business-media articles
- **Format sought:** thought-leadership articles from McKinsey, Bain, and others — **no free interactive diagnostic tool found from any of them**. This is content-rich, tool-poor: the big consultancies write extensively about why transformations fail but keep any actual diagnostic instrument behind a paid engagement.
- **Driver:** business pain, board-visible, often career-relevant for the transformation leader personally
- **Inputs an honest utility would need:** self-reported signals mapped to the well-documented failure patterns (executive support, employee resistance, budget-to-capability-building ratio, whether aspirations are fact-based)
- **Honest output:** which known failure patterns are present, not a success/failure prediction — genuinely defensible since it's pattern-matching against published research (McKinsey's own 72%-of-failures-from-two-factors finding), not a proprietary model claiming to predict your outcome
- **Crowded or underserved:** the single most underserved opportunity in this research pass. High-authority content exists everywhere; nobody has made it self-serve and interactive.

### Benefits not being realized
- **Exact language:** "benefits realization," "benefits realization management," specific stat: only 1 in 5 organizations report high benefits-realization maturity
- **Format sought:** trackers — but this space has **real, funded SaaS competitors** (Praxie, Sharktower) doing ongoing benefit tracking, plus many static Excel templates
- **Driver:** business pain, PMO/portfolio-governance-level
- **Crowded or underserved:** the ongoing-tracking use case is served by real competitors; the pre-project *diagnostic* moment (are this initiative's benefits even clearly enough defined to track in the first place?) is not. That distinction matters — don't compete with Praxie/Sharktower on tracking, occupy the moment before tracking would even be possible.

### Strategic initiatives missing targets
Largely the same territory as "initiatives underperforming" and "failed transformations" above — the language people use doesn't meaningfully separate this from those two themes.

### Loss of institutional or tribal knowledge
Carried forward from the second research pass — real, well-quantified pain (IDC's $2.5-3.5M/year estimate, Fortune 500's $31.5B/year figure), existing tools price replacing the person, not the reasoning that leaves with them. Still a strong, differentiated opportunity.

### Repeatedly revisiting work or decisions
Carried forward — confirmed again this round via the "duplication of effort" research above. The same root cause language keeps surfacing: lack of visibility into what already exists or was already decided.

### Resources committed to low-value initiatives
See "wasted project resources" above — same theme, same finding (crowded prioritization-tool space).

## Ranked strongest opportunities

Ranked against your six criteria this round: recognizable business pain, real search behavior, ability to produce a credible result, shareability with leadership, natural connection to Jaspen, differentiation from existing tools.

### 1. Transformation / Initiative Risk Diagnostic
The strongest finding of this pass. "Why do transformations fail" is some of the most widely read, most widely cited business content that exists (Bain, McKinsey, dozens of others), and despite that enormous content volume, nobody has turned the well-documented failure patterns into a free, self-serve diagnostic. Business pain is board-visible and often personal for the transformation leader. Credible result: yes, framed as pattern-matching against published research, not a success prediction. Shareability: very high — this is exactly the kind of finding someone brings into a steering committee. Jaspen connection: strong, and it gives the existing Initiative Readiness / Organizational Maturity ideas a much more universally-recognized name and hook to attach to.

### 2. Budget Overrun Exposure Estimator
Possibly the single most universally-feared project statistic there is, which makes it unusually shareable and recognizable. A competitor exists (budgetoverrun.com) but reads as generic. Real differentiation is available by tying overrun exposure to which specific project inputs are assumed versus evidenced — a mechanism only Jaspen's underlying methodology can honestly support. Credible result achievable as a reference-class range, not a prediction.

### 3. Benefits Realization Gap Check
Strong, well-documented pain (the "1 in 5" maturity stat is genuinely striking) and a real, specific white space: the pre-project diagnostic moment, distinct from the ongoing-tracking tools (Praxie, Sharktower) that already exist. High shareability at gate-review and PMO level. Very strong fit to Jaspen's own rigor about clearly defined, evidenced criteria.

### 4. Decision Confidence Checker / Evidence Sufficiency Checker (carried forward from the prior research pass)
Still holds up and is reinforced by this round's findings — assumed-not-evidenced numbers are the throughline connecting budget overruns, benefits that don't materialize, and initiatives that underperform. If anything, this pass strengthens the case for these rather than replacing them.

### 5. Decision Memory Gap / Decision Log Health Check (carried forward)
Reinforced again by this round's "duplication of effort" findings — the same root cause (no visibility into what was already decided or already done) keeps appearing across every theme researched in all three passes.

### 6. Initiative Readiness Score
Still valid, but this research suggests it may be better designed as one umbrella diagnostic with multiple output lenses (readiness, budget-overrun exposure, benefits-definition clarity) rather than three or four separate tools that all ask a similar set of upfront questions. Worth deciding deliberately rather than building each in isolation.

### 7. Institutional Knowledge / Decision-Continuity Risk Calculator (carried forward)
Still a real, differentiated opportunity; unchanged by this pass.

### 8. Duplicated Effort Cost Estimator
Real pain, genuine (if modest) white space for the cross-team/organizational version, distinct from the engineering-specific tools found in the first pass. Weaker Jaspen tie than #4 or #5, since it's more about visibility tooling than decision-making itself.

### 9. Low-Value Initiative / Portfolio Exposure Check
Real pain, but this is the most crowded space found across all three research passes (RICE, ICE, value-vs-complexity matrices, and dedicated tools already exist). Only worth pursuing if it's clearly built around evidence/confidence rather than another value-effort quadrant — otherwise not recommended.

## What this changes

- **The single best new idea across all three research passes is the Transformation / Initiative Risk Diagnostic.** It has the rare combination of enormous, proven content demand and a near-total absence of any free, interactive version of it — a gap this large is unusual to find.
- **Several previously-separate utility ideas (Initiative Readiness, Budget Overrun Exposure, Benefits Realization Gap) may be facets of one well-designed diagnostic rather than three or four separate builds.** Worth deciding this deliberately before any of them get built, rather than discovering the overlap mid-build.
- **The portfolio/prioritization utility idea should likely be dropped** — it's the most crowded space found in any of the three research passes, and a generic version wouldn't differentiate.
