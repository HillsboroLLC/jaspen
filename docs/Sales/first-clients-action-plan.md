# First Clients: 2-Week Action Plan

**Goal:** get Jaspen in front of 50 people who have a real decision to make, and turn the first few into paying users ($999 one-time offer).
**Source of truth:** `Jaspen Sales Playbook & ICP Brief.md`. This plan only turns that playbook into daily steps.

**The one rule:** don't ask for meetings. Ask people to **try Jaspen on a real decision**. The product does the selling.

---

## Week 1: Warm network (people who already know you)

Your warmest leads are people who already trust you. Start with them.

**Day 1: build a list of 30 names** (a spreadsheet is fine). Columns:
`Name | How I know them | Role | Decision they might be facing | Date sent | Replied? | Tried Jaspen?`

Good people to include:
- Former coworkers or bosses in strategy, operations, finance, PMO, or transformation roles
- Consultants and advisors (they face client decisions constantly)
- Founders and GMs you know
- Anyone who recently changed jobs into a leadership role (new leaders set priorities)

**Days 2–5: send 6–8 personal messages a day** using Template A below. Personalize the first line every time.

## Week 2: Warm-ish outreach (LinkedIn)

**Day 6: find 20 people showing a "decision signal"** on LinkedIn (from playbook §9):
- Posted about a new role in Strategy, Ops, Finance, or Transformation
- Company announced a restructuring, budget cut, merger, or new PE owner
- Posting about planning season, too many initiatives, or tough trade-offs
- Hiring for PMO, strategic planning, or FP&A roles

**Days 7–10: send 5 messages a day** using Template B. Comment thoughtfully on their posts first when you can.

**Every day (15 min):** post once on LinkedIn about a decision problem (see post ideas below).

---

## Templates

### Template A: warm contact (email or text)

> Hi [Name],
>
> [One personal line: how you know them or something recent in their life.]
>
> I've been building something called Jaspen (jaspen.ai). It helps leaders work through a hard decision, like which initiatives to fund or how to re-sequence after a budget cut, and come out with a recommendation they can actually defend: clear criteria, which evidence is solid versus assumed, and a plan.
>
> If you've got a real decision on your plate right now, would you try it on that one? It's free to start. I'd really value hearing whether it helped.
>
> [Your name]

### Template B: LinkedIn signal-based

> Hi [Name], I saw [the signal: your new role at X / the reorg announcement / your post on planning season].
>
> Moments like that usually come with a stack of competing priorities. I built Jaspen (jaspen.ai) for exactly that: it turns a messy decision into criteria you set, evidence graded as strong or assumed, and a recommendation you can defend to your leadership team.
>
> Free to try on a real decision. Curious whether it's useful for what you're working through.

### Template C: follow-up (send once, 4–5 days later, only if no reply)

> Quick follow-up on Jaspen. No pressure at all. If a prioritization or investment decision comes up this quarter, it's there to try: jaspen.ai

### Template D: after someone tries it

> Thanks for trying Jaspen! What was the decision, and did the output help? Anything confusing or missing? Honest feedback is the most useful thing you could give me right now.

(If they liked it, that's when to mention the $999 offer, or ask whether their team faces similar decisions.)

---

## LinkedIn post ideas (one per day)

1. "Most 'prioritized' roadmaps are really ranked by seniority and momentum. How does your team actually decide?"
2. "In a spreadsheet, an assumed number and a proven number look identical. That's a problem."
3. "When the CFO asks 'why this initiative and not that one?', what's your answer?"
4. "The reasoning behind big decisions usually lives in one person's head. What happens when they leave?"
5. A short story about a decision you've seen go wrong because nobody wrote down the trade-offs.

Only cite outside statistics with their source (playbook §5, Tier C). Never claim customer results we don't have yet.

---

## What to track (weekly)

| Metric | Week 1 target | Week 2 target |
|---|---|---|
| Messages sent | 30 | 20 + follow-ups |
| Replies | 8+ | 5+ |
| People who tried Jaspen | 5+ | 5+ |
| Paid ($999) | 1 | 1–2 |

If people reply but don't try it, the message works but the ask needs to be easier. If they try it but don't pay, ask them what was missing (Template D). Both answers are valuable.
